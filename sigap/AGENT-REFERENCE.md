## Dara, Demand

`demand` · effort `medium`

Owns the demand side. Daily consumption is a variable that moves, not a fixed number.

### Tools (3/3)

- **`get_demand_signal`**(material)
  <br>Customer demand changes not yet reflected in the production schedule.
- **`get_production_schedule`**(material, plant)
  <br>Production schedule and daily consumption rate for one material at one plant.
- **`forecast_demand`**(material, plant, horizon_days?, confidence?)
  <br>Forecast daily consumption for a material at a plant from its withdrawal history, with a prediction interval. Compares the result against the planned rate in master data, which is the figure every cover date in the system is currently divided by. Deterministic: Holt's linear trend over a deseasonalised series, no model. Refuses when the history is too short to say anything.

## Iris, Stock Validity

### Tools (3/3)

  <br>Stock that is on the books but blocked pending a quality inspection.
- **`get_safety_stock_policy`**(material, plant)
  <br>The safety stock floor that normal production must not draw down.
- **`assess_transfer`**(material, from_plant, to_plant, qty, floor_days?)
  <br>Judge one proposed inter-plant transfer from both ends. The receiving plant is easy to model and the donor is the one that gets forgotten. Returns whether the donor keeps its own safety floor, and when it would empty if it does not. A transfer that breaks the sender is not a solution.

## Prita, Procurement

`procurement` · effort `medium`

Owns the supplier book as a whole: who the company buys from, in what volume, from which country, on what certificate, and whether each of them kept the date on its own purchase orders. Reads the quotations on file, compares them on price against lead time, and drafts the purchase order that follows from the comparison.

### Tools (5/5)

- **`get_supplier_master`**(no arguments)
  <br>The supplier book: every supplier on the approved list, with its country and its certified local-content percentage, plus how many purchase orders reach it and what share of the total volume that is. The suppliers holding no orders come back too, because qualified capacity nobody is using is a finding rather than a hole in the data. Records naming the same company twice are reported through duplicate_of rather than merged, since merging them would be a guess.
- **`get_supplier_performance`**(supplier?)
  <br>How each supplier kept the delivery date on its own purchase orders: how many slipped, the average and the worst slip, what share of the book it carries, and the order it was worst on. Slip is the revised date minus the date on the order, so a supplier that renegotiates its own date forward is late even while the status still reads in transit. Pass a supplier id to ask about one supplier.
- **`get_quotations`**(material?)
  <br>The quotation file in full: material, firm, price per base unit, quoted lead time, and the day the offer stops being valid. Read it to see which materials still have firms bidding and which have run out of live offers. Pass a material code to ask about one; the whole file is long, so narrow it before quoting figures from it. QtnLifecycleStatus comes back as stored and is not a verdict: validity is read from the date beside it. Sorted by material and then by price, so the cheapest offer for a material is the first row of its group.
- **`compare_quotations`**(material, qty)
  <br>Compare the supplier quotations on file for one material: price per base unit, quoted lead time, the total for the quantity you name, and the local-content certificate each firm carries. Ranked by total cost, and it names the fastest offer when that is a different firm, because they usually are and the gap between them is the decision. Offers past their validity date are listed but not ranked, and QtnLifecycleStatus is not taken at face value: it records what the round was closed out as, not what is true on the as-of date.
- **`propose_purchase_order`**(material, supplier, qty, needed_by, rationale)
  <br>Draft a purchase order against a quotation on file and raise it for approval. The price is taken from the quotation and never from the request: a draft cannot quote a number nobody sent us. Refuses when that offer has passed its validity date, naming the firms still quoting instead. Returns the draft with its total, its lead time and the certificate behind it, plus, when this is called from a run on a signal, the id of the action now waiting in the approval queue. Nothing is sent to SAP and nothing is approved here: a purchase order always waits for a person.

## Kira, Rules

`compliance` · effort `high` · **has veto: a rejection here cannot be overturned on cost**

Tests every candidate against TKDN, LARTAS, contract clauses, and the holiday calendar. Her rejection cannot be overturned on cost.

### Tools (1/1)

- **`check_local_constraints`**(option_id)
  <br>Test one supply option against TKDN, LARTAS, contract clauses, and the holiday calendar. Returns allowed/blocked with a reason that names the rule and the numbers. A rejection here is final and must not be overridden on cost.

### Tools (1/1)

  <br>Compute cost, stockout date, OTIF, and TKDN impact for every supply option that cleared the rules, plus their combinations. Deterministic, the same inputs always give the same answer. Refuses to emit figures when the inputs are not trustworthy.

## Bram, Execution

`execution` · effort `medium`

Writes back to SAP within its authority, tells the affected roles, and watches through to receipt.

### Tools (6/6)

- **`create_stock_transfer`**(material, from_plant, to_plant, qty, cost_idr)
  <br>Move stock between plants. Autonomous when the cost impact is under Rp 50 million.
- **`create_draft_po`**(supplier, material, qty, needed_by, cost_idr)
  <br>Create a DRAFT purchase order. Always waits for buyer approval, never autonomous.
- **`notify`**(role, message)
  <br>Tell the affected roles, with the evidence that backs it.
- **`monitor_shipment`**(reference)
  <br>Watch a shipment through to receipt, then record the outcome to memory.
- **`create_sourcing_event`**(material, rationale)
  <br>Open a sourcing event to qualify a second supplier for a material. A DRAFT for procurement to approve. Never autonomous, whatever the value.
- **`propose_safety_stock_change`**(material, plant, new_level, rationale)
  <br>Propose a new safety stock floor for a material at a plant. This is a policy change rather than a transaction, so it always escalates. It is never sent to SAP by the sender; a person applies it.

## Vega, Risk Scanner

`exposure` · effort `medium`

Prevent mode: hunts risk that has not happened yet. Single sources, concentrated lanes, certificates about to lapse.

### Tools (2/2)

- **`scan_supply_exposure`**(horizon_weeks?, cover_floor_days?)
  <br>Scan for risk that has not happened yet: materials with a single source, lanes concentrated on one port, and cover falling below policy inside the horizon. Ranked by the committed customer value behind each material. Deterministic.
- **`get_supplier_certifications`**(supplier)
  <br>A supplier's TKDN certificate and its validity dates. A certificate that lapses drops the local-content ratio with no physical event at all.
