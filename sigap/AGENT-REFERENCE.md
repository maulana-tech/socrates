## Available to every agent

Owned by no agent on purpose: presentation is not a domain, so these
are appended to every agent's tool list.

- **`chart_finding`**(key, kind, x, y, title?, series?, limit?, rows?, order?)
  <br>Draw something already on the shared board as a chart, so the answer is read rather than parsed. Pass the board key of a finding you have ALREADY read, the kind of chart, and which two of its columns go on the axes. You cannot pass data: the figures come from the tool result under that key, and its origin label is carried onto the chart. Use it whenever an answer is a comparison across more than about four rows, or a movement over time. Prefer a chart AND one sentence reading it over a paragraph describing the numbers.

## Arya, Team lead

`supervisor` · effort `high`

Pulls specialists onto the team as the findings warrant, judges when the evidence is enough, and writes the recommendation with its trade-offs and residual risk.

### Tools (2/2)

- **`detect_disruption`**(event_id)
  <br>Take a disruption signal from an outside feed. In this application events arrive via POST /events, so this tool reads what has been recorded.
- **`summarise_period`**(days?)
  <br>The facts for a periodic operations report: what was handled, decided, approved and confirmed over a window, and separately where the position stands now. Deterministic, every number counted from the record. Use it to write a weekly or monthly summary. It gives you the figures; the judgement about what matters is yours, and you must not invent a number it did not return.

## Elsa, Impact

`impact` · effort `medium`

Traces a disruption through orders, stock, and the bill of materials to the customer commitments genuinely at risk.

### Tools (6/6)

- **`get_open_purchase_orders`**(port?, material?)
  <br>Open purchase orders, filterable by loading port or material.
- **`get_material_stock`**(material?, plant?)
  <br>Material stock per plant together with its daily consumption rate.
- **`get_bom_explosion`**(material)
  <br>Which finished goods consume this material.
- **`get_sales_order_commitments`**(finished_goods)
  <br>Customer orders for given finished goods, with their value.
- **`check_order_promise`**(finished_good, quantity, by_date, plant?)
  <br>Can this customer order be met, and if not, the earliest date it can. Walks the bill of materials down to every component, counts stock on hand net of what is already promised elsewhere plus supply in transit, and names the component that binds the date. Deterministic.
- **`propose_allocation`**(material, plant)
  <br>When supply cannot cover every customer order, propose which orders are served and which wait. Ranks by what missing an order costs per unit of the scarce material, and reports what plain due-date order would have cost instead, so the ranking carries a price rather than an opinion. Deterministic. Proposes only: the allocation is a commercial decision and needs a person.

## Dara, Demand

`demand` · effort `medium`

Owns the demand side. Daily consumption is a variable that moves, not a fixed number.

### Tools (3/3)

- **`get_demand_signal`**(material)
  <br>Customer demand changes not yet reflected in the production schedule.
- **`get_production_schedule`**(material, plant)
  <br>Production schedule and daily consumption rate for one material at one plant.
- **`forecast_demand`**(material, plant, horizon_days?, confidence?)
  <br>Forecast daily consumption for a material at a plant from its withdrawal history, with a prediction interval. Compares the result against the planned rate in master data, which is the figure every cover date in the system is currently divided by. Deterministic: Holt's linear trend over a deseasonalised series, no model. Refuses when the history is too short to say anything.

## Iris, Stock Validity

`inventory` · effort `medium`

Which stock may actually be used: quality holds, rejected batches, safety floors.

### Tools (3/3)

- **`get_quality_holds`**(material, plant)
  <br>Stock that is on the books but blocked pending a quality inspection.
- **`get_safety_stock_policy`**(material, plant)
  <br>The safety stock and reorder point for one material at one plant, computed from its measured daily use and its supplier's measured lead time against the service level in references/operating-targets.md. Returns the numbers and the reasons that produced them. Refuses when the withdrawal history is too short to size a cushion from.
- **`assess_transfer`**(material, from_plant, to_plant, qty, floor_days?)
  <br>Judge one proposed inter-plant transfer from both ends. The receiving plant is easy to model and the donor is the one that gets forgotten. Returns whether the donor keeps its own safety floor, and when it would empty if it does not. A transfer that breaks the sender is not a solution.

## Prita, Procurement

`procurement` · effort `medium`

Owns the supplier book as a whole: who the company buys from, in what volume, from which country, on what certificate, and whether each of them kept the date on its own purchase orders. Reads the quotations on file, compares them on price against lead time, and drafts the purchase order that follows from the comparison.

### Tools (6/6)

- **`get_supplier_master`**(no arguments)
  <br>The supplier book: every supplier on the approved list, with its country and its certified local-content percentage, plus how many purchase orders reach it and what share of the total volume that is. The suppliers holding no orders come back too, because qualified capacity nobody is using is a finding rather than a hole in the data. Records naming the same company twice are reported through duplicate_of rather than merged, since merging them would be a guess.
- **`get_supplier_performance`**(supplier?)
  <br>How each supplier kept the delivery date on its own purchase orders: how many slipped, the average and the worst slip, what share of the book it carries, and the order it was worst on. Slip is the revised date minus the date on the order, so a supplier that renegotiates its own date forward is late even while the status still reads in transit. Pass a supplier id to ask about one supplier.
- **`get_quotations`**(material?)
  <br>The quotation file in full: material, firm, price per base unit, quoted lead time, and the day the offer stops being valid. Read it to see which materials still have firms bidding and which have run out of live offers. Pass a material code to ask about one; the whole file is long, so narrow it before quoting figures from it. QtnLifecycleStatus comes back as stored and is not a verdict: validity is read from the date beside it. Sorted by material and then by price, so the cheapest offer for a material is the first row of its group.
- **`compare_quotations`**(material, qty)
  <br>Compare the supplier quotations on file for one material: price per base unit, quoted lead time, the total for the quantity you name, and the local-content certificate each firm carries. Ranked by total cost, and it names the fastest offer when that is a different firm, because they usually are and the gap between them is the decision. Offers past their validity date are listed but not ranked, and QtnLifecycleStatus is not taken at face value: it records what the round was closed out as, not what is true on the as-of date.
- **`propose_purchase_order`**(material, supplier, qty, needed_by, rationale)
  <br>Draft a purchase order against a quotation on file and raise it for approval. The price is taken from the quotation and never from the request: a draft cannot quote a number nobody sent us. Refuses when that offer has passed its validity date, naming the firms still quoting instead. Returns the draft with its total, its lead time and the certificate behind it, plus, when this is called from a run on a signal, the id of the action now waiting in the approval queue. Nothing is sent to SAP and nothing is approved here: a purchase order always waits for a person.
- **`review_replenishment`**(plant?, status?)
  <br>The daily ordering review: every position whose stock has fallen to or near its reorder point, with how much to order and why. Counts supply already in transit and stock already promised to another handling, so it does not order twice. Deterministic, and it names the positions it could not plan rather than leaving them out.

`compliance` · effort `high` · **has veto: a rejection here cannot be overturned on cost**

Tests every candidate against TKDN, LARTAS, contract clauses, and the holiday calendar. Her rejection cannot be overturned on cost.

### Tools (1/1)

  <br>Test one supply option against TKDN, LARTAS, contract clauses, and the holiday calendar. ## Tara, Costing

`simulation` · effort `medium`

Costs the options that cleared the rules, and their combinations. Deterministic: she calls engine/simulate.py rather than reasoning it out.

### Tools (1/1)

- **`simulate_scenario`**(material, plant, allowed_options?)
  <br>Compute cost, stockout date, customer late-penalty exposure and TKDN impact for every supply option that cleared the rules, plus their combinations. Arrival dates already include the import permit, supplier qualification and port dwell. Returns chosen (cheapest plan with no shortfall), cheapest_total (lowest cost plus penalty, which may deliberately accept a short delivery), compliance_cost (what obeying the rules cost when a struck-out option was cheaper) and do_nothing_penalty. Deterministic. Refuses to emit figures when the inputs are not trustworthy.

## Otto, Precedent

`precedent` · effort `medium`

Institutional memory: what was done last time something like this happened, and how it turned out. Two separate memories, and he never mixes them: this company's finished runs, and a library of documented real disruptions that happened to other companies.

### Tools (2/2)

- **`search_past_incidents`**(keywords)
  <br>Find comparable past incidents and how their handling ended.
- **`search_industry_precedents`**(keywords)
  <br>Find documented real-world disruptions comparable to this one, from the reference library rather than from this company's own history. Use it when search_past_incidents finds nothing, and to sanity-check an internal precedent against what the industry has already seen.

## Bram, Execution

`execution` · effort `medium`

Writes back to SAP within its authority, tells the affected roles, watches through to receipt, and records what actually happened so the next incident can learn from this one.

### Tools (7/7)

- **`create_stock_transfer`**(material, from_plant, to_plant, qty, cost_idr)
  <br>Move stock between plants. Autonomous only when the cost impact is under Rp 50 million AND every figure the decision rests on is trusted. A modelled or missing figure anywhere under it sends this to a person however cheap it is.
- **`create_draft_po`**(supplier, material, qty, needed_by, cost_idr)
  <br>Create a DRAFT purchase order. Always waits for buyer approval, never autonomous.
- **`notify`**(role, message)
  <br>Tell the affected roles, with the evidence that backs it.
- **`monitor_shipment`**(reference)
  <br>Watch a shipment through to receipt, then record the outcome to memory.
- **`record_outcome`**(action_id, outcome, detail?)
  <br>Close the loop on an action that was sent: what actually happened, and when. Writes it to the record so the next incident can learn from this one.
- **`create_sourcing_event`**(material, rationale)
  <br>Open a sourcing event to qualify a second supplier for a material. A DRAFT for procurement to approve. Never autonomous, whatever the value.
- **`propose_safety_stock_change`**(material, plant, new_level, rationale)
  <br>Propose a new safety stock floor for a material at a plant. This is a policy change rather than a transaction, so it always escalates. It is never sent to SAP by the sender; a person applies it.

## Vega, Risk Scanner

`exposure` · effort `medium`

Prevent mode: hunts risk that has not happened yet. Single sources, concentrated lanes, certificates about to lapse.

### Tools (2/2)

- **`scan_supply_exposure`**(horizon_weeks?, cover_floor_days?)
  <br>Scan for risk that has not happened yet: materials with a single source, lanes concentrated on one port, and cover falling below policy inside the horizon. Ranked by the committed customer value behind each material. Deterministic.
- **`get_supplier_certifications`**(supplier)
  <br>A supplier's TKDN certificate and its validity dates. A certificate that lapses drops the local-content ratio with no physical event at all.
