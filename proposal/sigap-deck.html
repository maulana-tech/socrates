01 — The problem

Three days of manual replanning, spent on a decision with a 24-hour window

PT Karya Presisi Nusantara — Tier-1 automotive component manufacturer, Karawang. Two plants, 340 active materials, 61% of raw material value imported through Tanjung Priok, just-in-time contracts with OEM delivery penalties.

Roughly fourteen times a year a material supply is disrupted. Each time the same sequence plays out:

  • A planner notices an inbound shipment is late
  • Opens three systems to find which purchase orders are affected
  • Checks stock by hand against a production plan in a spreadsheet
  • Calls procurement about alternatives
  • Two or three days later a decision is made — and the only option left is air freight at four times the cost
The bottleneck is not missing data. Every fact needed to make the decision already exists in S/4HANA.

The bottleneck is that connecting those facts into a decision is entirely manual — and it competes with the planner's day job.

Operating profile modelled on publicly documented Indonesian Tier-1 automotive patterns. Every deployment supplies its own from master data.

02 — What makes this an Indonesian problem

Substituting a supplier here is not a cost-and-lead-time calculation

Three local constraints routinely invalidate the mathematically optimal answer. A global optimiser gets them wrong, every time.

TKDN

Local content ratio. OEM supply contracts specify a floor — commonly 40%. A cheaper import can breach it and void the contract that the savings were meant to protect.

LARTAS

Import licensing. A new country of origin adds roughly ten working days before the first shipment can clear customs. Lead time is not just shipping time.

Priok dwell time

Port dwell at Tanjung Priok is variable, not a constant — and national holiday shutdowns freeze customs and logistics entirely.

This is the moat. A global vendor can copy an agent loop in a quarter. Encoding TKDN and LARTAS correctly takes local operators years — and the agent that ignores them produces answers a planner cannot use.

04 — How SIGAP thinks

A worked event: a typhoon closes Ningbo, China's largest cargo port, for six days

DETECT
Recognises Ningbo, a port it depends on, in the advisory. Supervisor
SCOPE
12 open POs worth Rp 8.4 billion route through Ningbo — 5 materials, 3 suppliers. Impact Analyst
IMPACT
Only one material is critical: M-4471 at plant KRW1 — 84 t on hand, 9.2 t/day draw, depleted 13 Sep. Replenishment PO slips 14 Sep → 22 Sep. Gap: 9 days.
QUANTIFY
Traces through the BOM to 3 OEM sales orders worth Rp 2.14 billion, penalty 0.5%/day capped at 10% — Rp 214 million exposure on top.
GENERATE
Constructs five candidates across three strategies — expedite, substitute, reallocate — without being told what the strategies are. Sourcing
FILTER
Eliminates two on grounds unrelated to price — local-content and import-licensing rules. Compliance ◆ veto
SIMULATE
Prices survivors on cost, arrival, on-time-in-full delivery, local content and FX — then tests them in combination, which is where the winning answer is found. Simulation
DECIDE
Recommends a combination over the obvious single answer, and states the residual risk it accepts. Supervisor
ACT
Stock transfer autonomous · purchase order drafted for buyer approval · roles notified with the evidence chain. Execution
VERIFY
Monitors to receipt, confirms no plant stocks out, writes the outcome to memory for the next event.

Design target: under 20 minutes, unattended, versus 2–3 days of manual replanning. Each step's question is chosen from the previous step's answer — nothing here is a fixed pipeline.

A supervised swarm of ten agents

↓ ADMITS · SPECIALISTS HAND OFF PEER-TO-PEER · ALL READ THE BLACKBOARD ↑

Traces the disruption to the commitments at risk

Demand & Consumption

Consumption is a variable, not a constant

Inventory Integrity

Which recorded stock is genuinely usable

Sourcing

Finds and qualifies alternative supply

Logistics & ETA

Arrival dates that hold — dwell, transhipment

Compliance ◆ VETO

TKDN · LARTAS · contract clauses. A rejection cannot be overruled by cost.

Simulation

Prices options and combinations. Deterministic by design.

Precedent

What was done before, and how it turned out

Execution

Writes back within authority · ⚠ approval gate

SHARED BLACKBOARD Every finding carries its provenance — live · cached · derived · modelled. Inventory Integrity hands a quality hold straight to Impact Analyst and forces the stockout date to be recomputed, without the supervisor mediating.

Nineteen tools across ten agents, on Amazon Bedrock with Strands Agents SDK swarm primitives under Bedrock AgentCore. It is a supervised swarm: the supervisor holds the goal and the audit trail a contract-bound business requires, while the specialists share context and route themselves. No two disruptions activate the same set of agents.

08 — Bounded autonomy

What the system may do alone — and where it stops

ActionAuthority
Read, analyse, simulate, recommendAutonomous
Stock transfer under Rp 50 million impactAutonomous, reported after
Raise a purchase orderDraft only — buyer approves
New supplier, or over Rp 500 millionEscalates to procurement lead
Anything breaching TKDN or LARTASBlocked by design

Bounded autonomy is a design requirement, not a limitation.

It is what makes the system deployable in a contract-bound manufacturing business, and it is what gets it past the internal resistance every automation project meets. The planner approves and stays accountable; the agent removes the three days of assembling the picture, not the judgement.

An agent that admits a trade-off is more credible than one that always sounds certain. SIGAP states the residual risk it accepts and escalates what it should not decide alone.

10 — Feasibility

Specified against live interfaces, not mocks

Every tool targets a real endpoint. The ERP layer is written to the SAP S/4HANA Cloud sandbox on SAP Business Accelerator Hub — real OData services with real payload shapes, pagination and error semantics, reachable today with a free API key — and reasoning to Amazon Bedrock. We ruled out a simulated backend deliberately: with a live sandbox freely available, a mock only creates work that has to be undone.

The constraint engine reads real Indonesian sources — Kemenperin's local-content register, INSW LARTAS classifications, the national holiday decree, Bank Indonesia's JISDOR rate, BMKG cyclone bulletins — ingested into a versioned rule pack, so a rule change is a data change.

Path to production is a tenant swap, not a rewrite: same OData client, different base URL and credentials.

Build status, stated plainly.

At submission this is a specification, not running software. Complete: the agent topology, twelve tool contracts against published SAP APIs, the constraint rule set, the worked scenario with its arithmetic, and the eval suite.

Implementation starts immediately — first milestone the Ningbo scenario end to end against the sandbox.

We would rather tell you exactly where we stand than demonstrate something built on invented data.

The only thing we model is the customer: plant list, consumption rates and contract penalty terms come from a company's own master data on day one. Nothing about the profile is shaped to make the agent succeed.

11 — How we prove it, and where we score

An eval suite written before the implementation

Ten disruption scenarios, each exercising a different reasoning route: supplier fire, customs hold, demand spike, tariff change, currency move, failed inspection batch, transhipment delay, holiday customs freeze, and a supplier losing its TKDN certification mid-plan.

They are written before the code, not after it — so the system cannot be tuned to pass them.

At Demo Day, the judges pick one at random.

An agent that only performs on a rehearsed script fails that test. Ours is being built to pass it.
