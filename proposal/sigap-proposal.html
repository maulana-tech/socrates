SIGAP — an autonomous supply‑disruption response agent for Indonesian manufacturers

TEAM [team name] UNIVERSITY [university] MEMBERS [name 1] · [name 2] · [name 3] DATE September 2026

When a port closes, an Indonesian manufacturer loses two to three days deciding what to do about it — and by the time the decision is made, the cheap options have expired. SIGAP is an agent that detects the disruption, traces it through purchase orders, stock and customer commitments, generates and simulates its own mitigation options against local regulatory constraints, and executes the approved plan in SAP.

01 — Problem

Three days of manual replanning, spent on a decision that has a 24‑hour window

PT Karya Presisi Nusantara (KPN) is a Tier‑1 automotive component manufacturer in Karawang, West Java, producing brake caliper and transmission housing assemblies for OEM customers under just‑in‑time delivery contracts. Two plants, 340 active materials, 61% of raw material value imported — predominantly through Tanjung Priok.

Roughly fourteen times a year, a material supply is disrupted: a typhoon closes a loading port, customs clearance stalls, a supplier declares force majeure. Each time, the same sequence plays out. A planner notices an inbound shipment is late. They open three systems to find out which purchase orders are affected. They check stock by hand against a production plan that lives in a spreadsheet. They call procurement to ask about alternatives. Two or three days later, a decision is made — and by then the only remaining option is air freight at four times the cost.

The bottleneck is not a lack of data. Every fact needed to make the decision already exists in S/4HANA. The bottleneck is that connecting those facts into a decision is entirely manual, and it competes with the planner's day job.

Why this is harder in Indonesia than the textbook version. Substituting a supplier here is not purely a cost-and-lead-time calculation. Three local constraints routinely invalidate the mathematically optimal answer:
  • TKDN (local content ratio) — OEM contracts specify a floor. A cheaper import can breach it.
  • LARTAS import licensing — a new country of origin adds ~10 working days before the first shipment can clear.
  • Port dwell time at Tanjung Priok — Indonesia's main container gateway — is variable, not a constant, and national holiday shutdowns freeze customs entirely.

The operating profile — plants, consumption rates, contract terms — is modelled on publicly documented Indonesian Tier‑1 automotive patterns; every deployment supplies its own from master data. The regulatory, market and weather data the agent reasons over is live. Assumptions are stated where used.

03 — Why existing tools do not close this gap

Dashboards report the problem; they do not decide

Supply chain visibility platforms surface the delayed shipment. They stop precisely where the hard work begins: nothing tells the planner which finished goods are affected, when the line actually stops, or which of six possible responses survives contact with TKDN rules.

Conventional chatbots and RAG assistants answer questions about documents. They cannot read live purchase orders, cannot simulate a plan, and cannot write a purchase requisition back into SAP.

What is missing is an actor rather than an informant — something that performs the planner's full reasoning chain autonomously: scope the impact, quantify it, invent the options, discard the illegal ones, price the survivors, recommend with reasoning, execute under approval, and verify the outcome.

That chain is a sequence of interdependent decisions where each step's result determines the next query. It is the canonical case for an agent, and it is what SIGAP does.

04 — Solution

A worked event: a typhoon closes Ningbo, China's largest cargo port, for six days

The trace below is the reasoning path SIGAP is designed to take on a single disruption, annotated with the specialist that owns each step. Nothing in it is scripted: the system is given only the disruption signal and read access to the data, and decides for itself which questions to ask, which specialist to ask them of, and when it has enough evidence to recommend. Every figure is derived from the dataset in the appendix, not asserted.

DETECT
Maritime advisory ingested: Ningbo–Zhoushan, China's largest cargo port, closed 8–13 Sep. Agent identifies the port as a lane it depends on. Supervisor → detect_disruption(sources=[maritime_advisory, weather, customs_bulletin])
SCOPE
Finds 12 open purchase orders worth Rp 8.4 billion routed through Ningbo, covering 5 materials from 3 suppliers. Impact Analyst → get_open_purchase_orders(port="CNNGB", status="not_shipped|in_transit")
IMPACT
Screens each material against stock and consumption. Only one is critical: M‑4471 (ADC12 aluminium ingot) at plant KRW1 — 84 t on hand, 9.2 t/day draw, depleted 13 Sep. Its replenishment PO 4500018872 (120 t) slips from 14 Sep to 22 Sep. Production gap: 9 days. Impact Analyst → get_material_stock · get_bom_explosion — Inventory Integrity hands back a 6 t quality hold, moving the date forward one day
QUANTIFY
Traces M‑4471 through the BOM to caliper assembly FG‑1120 and on to 3 OEM sales orders worth Rp 2.14 billion, carrying a 0.5%/day late penalty capped at 10% — a maximum penalty exposure of Rp 214 million on top of the revenue at risk.Impact Analyst
GENERATE
Constructs five candidate mitigations across three strategies — expedite the existing source, substitute a source, or reallocate internal stock — without being told what the strategies are. Sourcing → find_alternate_sources · Logistics & ETA prices each arrival against Priok dwell time
FILTER
Tests every candidate against local constraints and eliminates two on grounds unrelated to price — TKDN is Indonesia's local-content ratio, LARTAS its import-licensing regime. See the option table below. Compliance → check_local_constraints(...) # TKDN · LARTAS · holiday calendar · veto
SIMULATE
Prices the survivors on landed cost, arrival date, resulting on-time-in-full delivery (OTIF), local-content impact and FX exposure — then tests them in combination, which is where the winning answer is found. Simulation → simulate_scenario(plan) → {cost, otif, stockout_date, tkdn_ratio, fx_risk}
DECIDE
Recommends C + B over the obvious single answer, and states the residual risk it is accepting rather than hiding it.Supervisor, after Precedent confirms no comparable failure on this supplier
ACT
Issues the stock transfer autonomously (below its Rp 50 million authority limit) and raises the purchase order as a draft pending buyer approval. Notifies planner, buyer and QA with the full evidence chain. Execution → create_stock_transfer(...) · create_draft_po(...) ⚠ approval required · notify(...)
VERIFY
Monitors both shipments to receipt, confirms KRW1 and SBY1 never stock out, and writes the outcome to memory so the next Ningbo event starts from what was learned here.Execution → monitor_shipment

Design target: under 20 minutes, unattended, versus 2–3 days of manual replanning. To be measured against the eval suite during enablement.

06 — System architecture

A supervised swarm of ten agents, routed at runtime

SIGAP is ten agents. A supervisor holds the goal and the audit trail; nine specialists each own a decision domain, its data and its tools. The system is a supervised swarm rather than a pipeline on two counts: every specialist reads and writes one shared blackboard, and any specialist may hand off directly to a peer when it finds something in that peer's domain — without returning to the supervisor. The route is therefore decided at runtime, and no two disruptions activate the same set of agents.

SHARED BLACKBOARD
Every finding, with its provenance, is written where all agents can read it — which is how Inventory Integrity can hand a quality hold straight to Impact Analyst and force a stockout date to be recomputed, without the supervisor mediating. DynamoDB · Bedrock AgentCore Memory.

Nineteen tools across ten agents, each written to a published S/4HANA OData contract — API_PURCHASEORDER_PROCESS_SRV, API_MATERIAL_STOCK_SRV, API_SALES_ORDER_SRV, API_BILL_OF_MATERIAL_SRV, API_PRODUCTION_ORDER_2_SRV, API_INSPECTIONLOT_SRV — with writes through API_PURCHASEREQ_PROCESS_SRV and SAP Ariba Buying. Reasoning on Amazon Bedrock, orchestration on Strands Agents SDK swarm primitives under Bedrock AgentCore runtime, memory and observability; built with Kiro. TKDN, LARTAS, holiday and Priok dwell rules live as versioned reference documents beside the code, so the constraint an option died on can be read and audited.

09 — Feasibility and next steps

What exists now, and what the hackathon period delivers

Specified against live interfaces, not mocks. Every tool targets a real endpoint. The ERP layer is written to the SAP S/4HANA Cloud sandbox on SAP Business Accelerator Hub — real OData services with real payload shapes, pagination and error semantics, reachable today with a free API key — and reasoning to Amazon Bedrock. We ruled out a simulated backend deliberately: with a live sandbox freely available, a mock would only create work that has to be undone.

The constraint engine reads real Indonesian sources: TKDN certification status from the Kemenperin local-content register, LARTAS classifications from INSW, the national holiday decree, Bank Indonesia's JISDOR reference rate, and BMKG tropical-cyclone bulletins — ingested into a versioned rule pack rather than hard-coded, so a rule change is a data change.

The only thing we model is the customer. Plant list, consumption rates and contract penalty terms come from a company's own master data on day one; we have no customer yet, so we supply a representative profile. Nothing about it is shaped to make the agent succeed.

Build status, stated plainly. At submission this is a specification, not running software. What is complete: the agent topology, the nineteen tool contracts against published SAP APIs, the constraint rule set, the worked scenario with its arithmetic, and the eval suite. Implementation starts immediately, first milestone the Ningbo scenario end to end against the sandbox. We would rather tell you exactly where we stand than demonstrate something built on invented data.

Prior art. Open-source multi-agent replenishment systems fan work out to parallel specialists on a predetermined path. SIGAP differs where it matters here: the route is chosen at runtime. All code is our own.

How we will prove it is genuinely autonomous. The eval suite is specified — ten disruption scenarios — supplier fire, customs hold, demand spike, tariff change, currency move, a supplier losing its TKDN certification mid-plan — each exercising a different reasoning route — and each activating a different subset of the swarm. A demand spike wakes Demand & Consumption first and never calls Sourcing; a failed inspection batch starts at Inventory Integrity. They are written before the implementation, not after it, so the system cannot be tuned to pass them. At Demo Day we invite the judges to pick one at random: a rehearsed agent fails that test, and ours is being built to pass it.
